# ULTSQL License FAQ

## What is the ULTSQL license?

ULTSQL is licensed under the **Functional Source License, Version 1.1, MIT Future License (FSL-1.1-MIT)**.

The FSL is an established, standardized Fair Source / source-available license (stewarded by Sentry and used by companies like GitButler, Sentry, and others). It gives you complete access to the source code, permits free commercial and internal use, and automatically converts to the standard open-source **MIT License** two years after publication.

## Is ULTSQL open source?

ULTSQL is **source-available** upon release. Under the terms of FSL-1.1-MIT, it automatically converts into an OSI-approved **MIT License** on the second anniversary of its release.

## Can I use ULTSQL for free?

**Yes.** Permitted use of ULTSQL is 100% royalty-free.

You can freely use ULTSQL for:
- Learning, education, and research
- Personal projects
- Internal company tools and infrastructure
- Commercial desktop, mobile, embedded, and server applications
- Commercial SaaS applications that use ULTSQL as their underlying database

## Can I build and sell commercial software that uses ULTSQL?

**Yes.** You can embed ULTSQL inside your commercial applications, products, and SaaS systems without paying any licensing fees or royalties. You can charge your customers for your software.

## Is there any forced attribution (e.g. “Powered by ULTSQL”)?

**No.** Under FSL-1.1-MIT, there is no requirement to display "Powered by ULTSQL" badges or splash screens in your user interface. You only need to retain standard copyright notices in source distributions as customary.

## What is the only restriction? (The "Competing Use" clause)

You cannot take ULTSQL and offer it as a **competing commercial database-as-a-service (DBaaS)** or managed cloud database service that directly substitutes for ULTSQL.

### Permitted examples:
- ✅ Building an e-commerce platform using ULTSQL as its database
- ✅ Building an AI search engine powered by ULTSQL vector search
- ✅ Bundling ULTSQL inside an on-premise enterprise software appliance
- ✅ Running ULTSQL on AWS EC2, Google Cloud, or Azure VMs for your own apps

### Prohibited without a commercial agreement:
- ❌ Offering a hosted "ULTSQL Cloud DBaaS" that sells managed ULTSQL instances to third parties in competition with the author.

## When does it convert to MIT?

Every release automatically and irrevocably converts to the standard permissive **MIT License** exactly **2 years** after its release date. Once converted, the competing use restriction ends for that version.
